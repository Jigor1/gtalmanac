CyberLeek, the anonymous figure who has been publishing stolen GTA 6 material since August (the official title is Grand Theft Auto VI), released a roughly 25-minute video titled "An Extended Leek" on October 8, 2026, the longest one so far. It shows gameplay of Jason and Lucia, story spoilers and explicit nudity. Rockstar has not commented and nobody has independently verified that the material is authentic. The "An Extended Leek" leak of October 8, 2026, according to the outlets that watched it. Editorial illustration, no frame from the video. 
The saga of leaks goes on, and this time it is the long format's turn. CyberLeek's first clips, which appeared on August 18, ran a minute or a little more, and by August 26 they had grown to more than fifteen gameplay snippets plus three images of the map. Always in small doses, with a token and a poll marking every drop. "An Extended Leek" breaks that rhythm and puts everything in a single file to download in one go. 
The numbers on the file do not match from one outlet to the next. Gameranx reports 367 MB and a runtime between 25 and 26 minutes, Dexerto mentions a ZIP archive above 370 MB and MP1st about 387 MB. None of the three explains the gap and none could establish where the material comes from. Every detail below should therefore be read as reported by the sources, not as confirmed. 
What does "An Extended Leek" show? 
"An Extended Leek" is a video of about 25 minutes with gameplay of Jason and Lucia, the two protagonists of GTA 6. According to the outlets that watched it, it contains missions, cutscenes, races in Vice City, a clothing store and a mud race, along with spoilers and explicit nudity. 
The most detailed account comes from MP1st. Jason and Lucia arrive together at the Thrillbilly Mud Club for a mud race, then come a clothing store, a car theft with decal changes in a customized garage and a mission with Jason and an NPC (non-player character) without Lucia. The nudity arrives around the ten-minute mark. Dexerto adds the races in Vice City and reports Jason crossing the game world with no clothes on, with Lucia in bikini bottoms. 
For Game Rant (the piece is republished by OpenCritic) this is the longest gameplay leak attributed to CyberLeek, and the mud club scene is read as an example of "emergent dates" between the two protagonists. Insider Gaming opens its article with a warning about major spoilers, describes the material as "clearly recent" and says Take-Two keeps having it taken down from platforms. 
This page carries no link to the video and no frame. The material contains explicit nudity and gives away parts of the plot, and an archive of nearly 400 MB uploaded by an anonymous person remains a real risk for anyone who opens it (malware included). 
What changes compared with the August leaks? 
Compared with August, the format changes, with a single 25-minute video instead of clips of one to five minutes, and the question of the build, meaning the development version the material comes from, is open again. 
Back in the summer the sources already disagreed. TechSpot had spoken of gameplay taken from an older build, while HotHardware described the footage as close to the finished version without being able to say how close to the November 19 release. Today Insider Gaming talks about recent material, while MP1st and Dexerto name no build at all. A picture that, in practice, allows no conclusions. 
Then there is the ritual of the eve. On October 5, according to Dexerto, a poll with question marks had appeared, closing on October 8, the day of the video. Gameranx adds that after the release CyberLeek launched another one on its own domain. It is the same mechanism we reconstructed in the article on the memecoin that pays for GTA 6 leaks . In August the $CYBERLEEK token, created around the 15th of the month on the Solana network, gave holders the right to vote on which video came out first, and the watermark carried the slogan "HIGHER MARKETCAP = MORE LEEKS". 
Why 25 minutes weigh more than ten clips 
A one-minute clip lives for two hours on social media and then fades. A 367 MB archive, by contrast, needs a host, generates links that buckle under demand (Gameranx describes exactly this) and produces, in the following hours, a barrage of written recaps listing the scenes. It is those recaps, not the file, that travel furthest. Even people who never downloaded anything end up knowing what happens in the mission without Lucia or at the decal garage. 
For anyone waiting for the game, the damage is the spoiler, and with six weeks to November 19 the risk is high. It makes sense to mute the words "Extended Leek" and "CyberLeek" on X, Reddit and YouTube, plus the names of the locations cited by the outlets, because fragments circulate without warnings. For Rockstar the cost is different. Every minute of unauthorized gameplay competes with the material the company is releasing in a trickle, from the police that remembers cars and clothes to clarifications on the technical side, such as the one from Xbox that GTA 6 will not be playable on PC through Xbox Cloud Gaming . 
Crypto and donations, what is actually known 
On the money side the sources diverge, and part of what circulates is unconfirmed. Dexerto reports that CyberLeek promoted a cryptocurrency alongside the videos. A related headline on the site mentions more than $250,000 collected with a memecoin before Netflix's Extended Look, but the body of the article does not verify the figure. 
On donations, the only report we found is the post from the Instagram account worldytech, according to which at the end of the video CyberLeek asks the community for contributions to an unspecified project. Gameranx points to a related headline about a fundraiser for a "gaming related" project, but none of the articles we read carries the request in its own text. It should be treated as a claim awaiting confirmation. 
The stated motive remains protest. Back in August, HotHardware reported, CyberLeek asked Rockstar and the other publishers to give up a digital-only future and the removal of content already purchased. The same outlet judged a "rug pull" (abandoning the crypto project after cashing out) likely and saw nothing altruistic in the leaker's moves. That is one author's opinion, not a fact, but it carries weight because it comes from someone who has followed the case from the start. 
Rockstar and Take-Two, no comment for now 
Rockstar, for its part, has not publicly commented on the October 8 video, at least as of the writing of Dexerto's article, and none of the outlets consulted cites a Take-Two statement on the new material. Game Rant only recalls that the two companies are hunting for those responsible, with investigations reaching Discord and Xbox. 
The legal story does have a starting date, though. On August 20 Take-Two filed two court requests against Microsoft and Discord in New York to identify the anonymous leaker, the move we covered in the piece already cited. On August 26 Rockstar called the leaks "heartbreaking" and confirmed the November 19 release ( the official response ), and the next day it answered in its own way with the 26 minutes of the Extended Look on Netflix . The title the leaker chose plays on the official one, with "Leek" in place of "Look".

According to Gameranx, after Rockstar's official video and the end of the embargo on interviews and marketing, the weight of the leaks had diminished. October 8 puts that reading to the test. The long video does not move the release date, at least going by what has been reported so far, but it forces Rockstar to decide whether to keep quiet or to answer again with a public statement like the one in August, having already handed the harshest part to its lawyers. 
If you want to follow the story from the beginning, the first gameplay and map leak , which appeared nine days before the Netflix reveal, remains the starting point. 
Frequently asked questions (FAQ) 
1. Is the CyberLeek video authentic? 
Nobody has verified it independently. Rockstar has neither confirmed nor denied it, and Dexerto states that the authenticity remains unverified. The takedowns requested by Take-Two, as reported by Insider Gaming, suggest the company claims rights over the material, but they do not show where it comes from or whether it is a recent build. 
2. Where can you watch "An Extended Leek"? 
We neither publish nor link the video. It contains explicit nudity and spoilers, and whoever spread it is anonymous. The outlets consulted report that the download links gave out within hours under demand and that Take-Two keeps having it removed. Downloading an archive of nearly 400 MB from an unknown source also exposes you to malware. 
3. Does the GTA 6 release date change? 
No source reports any delay. Rockstar confirmed November 19, 2026 on August 26, after the first leaks, and the October 8 coverage keeps citing that date. A delay caused by a leak would still be a company decision, not an automatic consequence of the video. 
4. Who is CyberLeek? 
An alias, not an identified person. None of the sources consulted gives a name. Take-Two asked Microsoft and Discord for the data to trace the author, and until a public outcome of those requests arrives, CyberLeek remains a brand tied to a crypto token and to a series of serialized releases. 
Six weeks to launch 
Six weeks to November 19 and a leaker who publishes whenever he likes. Rockstar, for now, speaks through the August statements and through its lawyers. A script we have seen before, except that this time the episode runs 25 minutes.
